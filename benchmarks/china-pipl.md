# Benchmark: China Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL)

## Purpose and applicability

China’s Personal Information Protection Law (PIPL) governs personal-information processing within its scope. Cross-border processing, localization, security assessment, certification, and standard-contract requirements may also be affected by other Chinese laws, regulations, and current implementing measures. Applicability depends on the processing and parties; obtain advice from qualified counsel with relevant China expertise.

## Hospitality assessment topics

For relevant operations, consider:

- Processing purposes, necessity, notice, consent or other applicable legal basis, and individual rights.
- Sensitive personal information, including data that can create heightened harm; assess any use of identity, biometric, precise location, or health-related data.
- Separate consent or additional safeguards where legally required.
- Processor and entrusted-processing arrangements, third-party sharing, and platform integrations.
- Cross-border transfers, data localization, security assessments, and other transfer mechanisms as applicable.
- Protection impact assessments and records where required.
- Security, retention, incident handling, and responsible-person governance.

## Evidence examples

Data maps, notices and consent records, purpose/necessity assessments, processor contracts, transfer records, impact assessments, access controls, retention rules, and incident procedures.

## Assessment caveat

This document is an issue-spotting aid, not legal advice. Requirements and implementation measures can change and may depend on data volumes, sector, critical information infrastructure status, transfer facts, and regulatory guidance. Confirm the current position with qualified counsel and official sources.
