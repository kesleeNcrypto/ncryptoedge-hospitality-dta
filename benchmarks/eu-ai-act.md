# Benchmark: EU Artificial Intelligence Act

## Purpose and applicability

Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (the EU AI Act) establishes a risk-based framework for AI systems and general-purpose AI models. Applicability and obligations depend on the provider/deployer role, system purpose, risk classification, placing on the market or use in the EU, and the regulation’s phased application. Confirm current implementation dates and guidance before relying on this benchmark.

## Hospitality assessment topics

Inventory AI systems and features, including chatbots, translation, recommendation, dynamic pricing, identity or biometric features, workforce tools, fraud detection, and guest profiling. For each, document:

- Intended purpose, provider/deployer/importer/distributor roles, versions, and affected users.
- Risk classification rationale and whether prohibited-practice or high-risk provisions may be relevant.
- Human oversight, transparency, accuracy, robustness, cybersecurity, logging, and incident processes appropriate to the system and role.
- Data provenance, privacy interactions, vendor terms, change management, and monitoring after deployment.
- Staff and guest communications, escalation to a human, and accessible alternatives.
- Governance for general-purpose AI services, prompts, outputs, and sensitive information.

## Hospitality-specific caution

Do not assume that a system is low-risk solely because it is marketed as an assistant. Employment, access, safety, biometric, eligibility, or other consequential uses may require specific legal classification and safeguards. Avoid entering guest or employee personal or confidential data into third-party AI services unless approved and governed.

## Evidence examples

AI inventory, intended-purpose statements, provider documentation, classification assessments, impact assessments, contracts, notices, human-oversight procedures, test/monitoring records, and incident logs.

## Assessment caveat

This checklist is not legal advice or a classification decision. Confirm the consolidated legal text, phased application dates, Commission guidance, and national implementation arrangements with qualified counsel.
